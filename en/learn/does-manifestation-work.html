Does Manifestation Actually Work?

What holds up, what carries from the well known teachers, and where the limits are · Updated August 4, 2026

Almost everyone who starts asks this quietly, because they expect one of two answers: a lecture from one side, a sales pitch from the other. Neither is here.

The short answer

Yes, part of it works, and that part is well studied. It is the part where you do something with yourself: getting clear, writing it down, setting the next step, shifting your attention, staying kind with yourself. There are meta analyses with thousands of participants behind that, and the effects are real.

What is not supported is the explanation usually attached: that thoughts or written sentences attract outside events. No study shows that.

That sounds like a rejection but it is closer to relief. You do not have to believe anything for the exercises to work. They work through attention, clarity and action, and that is measurable.

What measurably works

Five exercises from this field are properly studied. In all five, the effect happens inside you.

1. Setting the next step

A sentence shaped like „When this situation happens, then I do exactly that". With a trigger, a place and an action, not a general resolution. Researchers call it an implementation intention.

Gollwitzer and Sheeran found a clear effect on follow through across 94 independent tests with more than 8000 people in 2006, d = 0.65. That analysis did not correct for publication bias. A newer review from 2024 across 642 tests lands on a range of d = 0.27 to 0.66. The range is the honest number, and even its lower end is a real effect.

In Enara: there is no dedicated tool for this step yet. Neither the morning ritual nor any writing prompt asks about the obstacle or a when then sentence. You write it yourself in the journal today. That is the largest known gap in the app, and it is here so you know before you download.

2. Writing about a good future

Describing in detail what your life looks like when a lot has gone well. Researchers call it the Best Possible Self exercise, studied by Laura King from 2001, first with 81 students over four days of twenty minutes each. A review across 29 studies with 2909 participants finds small to moderate effects on mood and optimism.

The caveat nobody includes: it depends on the comparison. Against a waitlist the effect looks large, against another active writing exercise it shrinks considerably, and tested directly against gratitude exercises almost nothing remains. Good exercise, not a better one. And mood was measured, not whether the future arrives.

In Enara: the writing prompt „Your day five years from now" is exactly this exercise, with an opening line and follow up questions.

3. Writing about one of your values

Not about a goal, but about something that matters to you, and why. This is the best supported exercise in the entire field. A 2015 meta analysis by Epton and colleagues across 144 experimental tests finds small but reliable effects on how open people are to unwelcome information and what they do afterward.

Important distinction: this is not support for sentences like „I am wealthy". What was studied is writing about values, not asserting states. Citing this research for affirmations is citing it wrong.

In Enara: in the free journal, any time. A guided prompt for it is built but not yet in the version currently on the store.

4. Writing down the heavy things

Twenty minutes about something weighing on you, no attention to form, no reader in mind. The most studied writing method there is, hundreds of randomized studies.

And still modest: Frattaroli's large review across 146 studies finds a meaningful but small overall effect, r = 0.075, with strong variation. It works, it works far better for some situations than others, and it is not therapy.

In Enara: the free journal, spoken instead of typed if you prefer.

5. Gratitude, looking back

Three lines about something that actually happened today. Small and specific. Emmons and McCullough found higher wellbeing across three studies in 2003.

More modest than its reputation: the 2021 meta analysis by Cregg and Cheavens finds a small effect that shrinks further once tested against another active exercise rather than a waitlist.

In Enara: a fixed step in the morning ritual, plus the evening review.

The well known teachers, and what carries

Most people arrive at manifestation through a name, not a study. So here are the six best known, each with their method and with what can actually be supported.

Neville Goddard

Lectures and writings from the 1940s onward. His core: do not ask for something, be the person who already has it. His best known exercise is a short scene that only makes sense if the wish were fulfilled, repeated quietly until sleep. A second is revision: going through the day again in the evening the way it should have gone.

Evidence: doctrine. What carries from the scene is what the Best Possible Self research shows, clarity and mood. One limit is not negotiable: Goddard justifies working on a specific person by claiming others are only your own assumption projected outward. That reasoning erases other people's self determination. Enara never works on a named person.

In Enara: „The conversation that changes everything" and „The perfect ordinary day" are Goddard's method in prompt form.

Rhonda Byrne, „The Secret"

The 2006 book that made the scene mainstream. Byrne's core point here: the power is in the feeling you put into the words, not in the words. Hence gratitude in advance, thanking as though it had already arrived.

Evidence: doctrine, in its riskiest form. What carries is the part supported gratitude also has: a deliberate shift of attention. What does not carry is the causal claim. And one statement in the book is simply false: that illness cannot exist in a body with harmonious thoughts. It is medically untenable and it produces guilt in sick people. More on that under the limits below.

In Enara: the form yes, the explanation no. There is a prompt called „Gratitude in advance", but Enara never says that gratitude causes anything.

Abraham Hicks and Louise Hay

Both work from the same smart idea: a sentence too far from where you are does not work, it creates resistance. So you do not take the biggest sentence, you take the next one you can believe. Instead of „I love myself", try „I am learning to be kinder to myself".

Evidence: doctrine. The Hicks material is channeled by its own account. There was a finding that served as empirical backing for years (Wood and colleagues 2009: people with low self esteem felt worse after repeating „I am a lovable person"). In 2020 that finding failed to replicate in two preregistered attempts with larger samples. The ladder idea is still a good design decision, it is just not research backed.

What Enara explicitly leaves out: in the Hicks framework the scale comes with the assumption that feelings attract events. That makes a bad feeling dangerous and the person partly responsible for what happens to them. We drop that part.

In Enara: each of the 720 sentences across twelve life areas comes in three strengths, from a cautious bridge to the full sentence. You pick the one that holds today.

Roxie Nafousi

The best known voice of the younger generation. For her gratitude is the engine rather than decoration, and she puts more weight on self worth and on action than the older literature does.

Evidence: the gratitude exercises are supported, see above, if more modestly than their reputation. The attraction explanation is not.

Joe Dispenza

The only figure in this scene having measurements taken at scale, which makes him the most interesting case. There are published studies around his multi day meditation retreats.

The strongest is a randomized study by Dawson Church and colleagues from 2022 with 513 participants and a real comparison group that read poetry instead of meditating. Among other things they measured an immune marker in saliva, which rose 49.5 percent in the meditation group, a faster path into alpha waves on EEG, and improvements in anxiety and pain. The stress hormone cortisol, by contrast, did not change meaningfully.

What that shows and what it does not: the study measures what intensive inner work does in the body. It did not measure whether wishes or life goals arrive. The authors name their own limits: not all participants were biologically tested, there was no six month follow up on the biology, and mood can be contagious in a group. Dispenza is not an author of this work, it studies his format.

So Dispenza is not a counterargument. He is the best illustration of this article's dividing line: that something measurably changes in the person is shown. That the world changes as a result is not.

In Enara: what we take from this corner is daily repetition and a calm frame, not the healing claims. And we deliberately keep these results away from our app: a seven day retreat is not a journal on your phone.

The finding that turns the whole thing around

One result belongs to everyone who manifests, because it makes the practice work better.

Gabriele Oettingen and Doris Mayer published four studies in 2002: graduates entering the job market, students with a crush, students facing an exam, and patients recovering from hip surgery. Across all four, the people with the most positive fantasies had the worse real outcomes: fewer job offers, lower starting salaries, fewer relationships started, slower recovery.

The consequence is small and it changes everything: the picture can stay, it just needs two more sentences. What is in the way, and when you take the first step. What that looks like in practice is in the scripting guide.

Where the limits are

Three places, and none of them is a matter of opinion.

Illness. No sentence and no journal replaces treatment. The best known documented case is Kim Tinkham, who declined guideline based treatment for her breast cancer after a television program about „The Secret" and died of the disease.

Money. Dixon, Hornsey and Hartley published three studies with 1023 people combined in 2023. People who believed more strongly in manifestation expected unusually large success in unusually little time, leaned toward riskier investments and had experienced bankruptcy more often. They found no evidence of actually greater success. The work is correlational, it shows what occurs together. Practically: no picture of fast wealth belongs next to a pending money decision.

The inference back onto the person. If thoughts attract everything, then people in grief, in poverty or after violence attracted their misfortune. That conclusion is false, and it always lands on whoever is already carrying the most. If what stands in your way is outside you, it is outside you. Then the wish gets smaller, not the person guilty.

Are you making a fool of yourself?

No. What you are doing when you manifest is, at its core, taking time for the question of what you actually want and writing it down. That a doctrine with claims it cannot deliver has grown around that handful of minutes changes nothing about the minutes.

Take the exercises and leave the promises. Measure success not by whether the universe delivers, but by whether you know more clearly what you want.

What you do tomorrow

Take a wish that feels close. Write half a page of scene, present tense, two senses, one named feeling. Then two sentences underneath: what is in the way, and when you take the first step. Ten minutes.
